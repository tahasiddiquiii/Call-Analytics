Question 2 of 3: how did the customer feel, and how did the call end?

Feelings scale: very_negative (angry, threatening to leave or complain, accusing, feels misled), negative (annoyed, worried, frustrated, disappointed), neutral (calm, or reluctant acceptance such as "Fine. Thanks, I guess."), positive (satisfied, thankful), very_positive (delighted, praising).

customer_first_words: copy, word for word, the customer's first sentence about their reason for calling.
sentiment_start: the customer's feeling in their first two turns.
customer_last_words: copy, word for word, the customer's last sentence in the call.
sentiment_end: the customer's feeling in their LAST two turns only, not earlier in the call.
sentiment_overall: the customer's feeling across the whole call.

Answer these three questions with true or false:
handed_over: did the agent pass the problem to someone else (a supervisor, a technician or a specialist team) with an escalation, a ticket or a promised callback, because the agent could not solve it themselves? It is false if the customer is told to contact someone themselves.
solution_agreed: did the customer end the call with a real solution they accepted? It is false if nothing was solved, if the customer must contact someone else to get an answer, or if the only "solution" was a promise the agent had no authority to make or took back.
needs_future_step: does the agreed solution still depend on something that happens after the call, such as a delivery, a scheduled check or visit, or a condition the customer set? A refund already submitted or a change already made does not count.

outcome_detail: one or two sentences on what was agreed, or why nothing was. Include amounts, days, conditions and reference numbers.
outcome_quote: copy, word for word, the transcript words that best show how the call ended (at most 20 words).
